The Higgs boson is produced at the LHC primarily through four mechanisms: gluon-gluon fusion (ggF), vector boson fusion (VBF), associated production with a vector boson (VH), and associated production with top/bottom quarks (ttH/bbH). Of these, ggF ($pp \rightarrow H$) is the dominant production mechanism. Although the Higgs boson does not couple directly to gluons since they are massless, it can be produced via a loop of virtual quarks. This loop is predominantly mediated by top quarks since the Higgs boson couples to fermions with a strength proportional to their mass. The top quark is by far the heaviest known fermion, so it has the largest contribution to the ggF process.

VBF ($pp \rightarrow qqH$) follows with a cross section about an order of magnitude smaller. In this process a quark from each proton emits a $W^{\pm}$ or $Z$ boson and the two vector bosons fuse to produce a Higgs boson. The resulting final state is characterized by two high energy forward jets. 

The VH (Higgsstrahlung) process ($pp \rightarrow WH$ and $pp \rightarrow ZH$) occurs with a similar cross section to VBF\@. In these events quarks from colliding protons annihilate to produce an off-shell vector boson, which then radiates a Higgs boson. $WH$ production is more common than $ZH$ due to the presence of two $W$ bosons and one $Z$ boson, as well as $W$ bosons having a stronger couplings to quarks since the $Z$ boson quark coupling depends on the electric and hypercharges.

Finally, ttH/bbH production ($pp \rightarrow t\bar{t}H$ and $pp \rightarrow b\bar{b}H$) is of the same order of magnitude as $ZH$. These events occur when gluons from each colliding proton produce a $t\bar{t} \slash b\bar{b}$ pair through pair production, and then one $t \slash b$ from each proton fuse into a Higgs boson leaving a final state with two top/bottom quarks and a Higgs boson. 

For this thesis, the production mode of interest is the $VH$ production mode with a focus on the $WH$ channel where the $W$ decays leptonically. Table~\ref{tab:higgs_production_cross_sections} summarizes the production cross sections for each mechanism at $\sqrt{s} = 13.6$ TeV.
