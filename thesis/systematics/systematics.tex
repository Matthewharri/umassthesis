This analysis is affected by several sources of systematic uncertainty, which fall into two categories.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Experimental systematic uncertainties:} related to reconstruction, identification, and trigger efficiencies. These are further subdivided into:
        \begin{itemize}
          \item \textbf{Four-momentum systematic uncertainties}, where object kinematics (e.g.\ muon momentum) are varied. These shifts affect the selection and therefore the final event yields in the SR and CRs. The variations follow the standard $\pm1\sigma$ prescription.
          \item \textbf{Scale Factor systematics}, where only the event weight is varied based on corrections derived from data-MC simulation comparisons.
        \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Theoretical systematic uncertainties:} associated with the simulation of signal and background processes.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experimental uncertainties}\label{sec:experimental_systematics}
Experimental systematic uncertainties are estimated using recommendations from the relevant ATLAS Combined Performance groups. The impact of scale and resolution uncertainties on energy and momentum measurements is evaluated by applying $\pm1\sigma$ variations to the relevant quantities (electron and jet energy or muon momentum), propagating them through the full selection, and observing the change in yields. These variations are referred to as smearing for resolution systematics.

Electrons have uncertainties associated with reconstruction, identification, isolation and trigger efficiencies which are determined from differences between data and simulation using tag-and-probe measurements in $Z\to e\bar{e}$ events. Additional uncertainties related to the energy scale and resolution are derived by varying the electron identification criteria from Medium to Tight, removing the isolation requirement, and applying a cut on the momentum fraction lost to bremmstrahlung within the ID. These uncertainties are described in more detail in Refs.~\cite{ATLAS:2023mnw, ATLAS:2023dxj}.

Muons have analogous uncertainties for reconstruction, identification, isolation, and trigger efficiencies, also derived from tag-and-probe measurements in $Z \to \mu\bar{\mu}$ events. Additional contributions arise from the efficiency of track-to-vertex association, as well as from reconstruction in the low-$p_T$ (SFs derived from $J/\psi$ decays) and high-$p_T$ (SFs derived from $Z$ decays) regimes. Further uncertainties are associated with the sagitta of the muon trajectory, which quantifies the deviation of the track from a straight line due to the magnetic field. These include charge-dependent variations in the momentum scale, statistical uncertainties on the charge-dependent corrections, transverse-momentum-dependent variations capturing non-sagitta effects in the end-caps, and any residual global charge-dependent momentum bias remaining after calibration. These uncertainties are described in more detail in Ref.~\cite{ATLAS:2020auj}.

Jet systematic uncertainties are primarily associated with JES and JER\@. The JES uncertainties are derived from in situ calibrations using $Z/\gamma+$ jet and multijet balance (MJB) events, which account for assumptions in topology, MC modeling, MC sample statistics, as well as propagated uncertainties from electron, muon, and photon energy scales. Additional JES contributions come from pile-up (NPV, $\mu$, $\rho$), flavor composition (light quark / heavy quark / gluon-initiated jets), and the energy scale in $b$-tagged jets. Further uncertainties arise from JVT, fJVT, and $b$-tag efficiency differences between data and MC simulation, derived from $Z\to \mu\bar{\mu}$+ jet events. These uncertainties are detailed in Ref.~\cite{ATLAS:2017bje}.

Systematic uncertainties in \met{} are more complex, as they depend on all objects contributing to \met{} reconstruction, affecting both the hard and soft terms. Hard term uncertainties are obtained by propagating the uncertainties of each contributing object through the \met{} reconstruction. Uncertainties among objects of the same type are considered correlated, while those between different object types are treated as uncorrelated due to independent reconstruction and calibration. The soft-term uncertainty is evaluated in events with negligible true \met{}, such as $Z\to e\bar{e}$, and validated in $Z\to \mu\bar{\mu}$ events. It is quantified by projecting the soft term onto the hard term, allowing separation into scale and resolution uncertainties. Since contamination from other events dominate above 100~GeV, the soft term uncertainty at 100~GeV is applied to all higher \pt{} events. More details are available in Ref.~\cite{ATLAS:2024cmj}.

A full list of experimental systematic sources considered in this analysis is provided in Table~\ref{tab:expSyst}.

\section{Theoretical uncertainties}\label{sec:TheorySystematics}

The theoretical uncertainties in the \wh{} channel are evaluated in the SRs at the level of the ANN discriminant. To further constrain uncertainties associated with the $WZ/W\gamma^{*}$ background, dedicated CRs are used for events with 0, 1, and $\geq$~2 jets.

Uncertainties in the \wh{}, diboson and triboson processes are estimated by varying the QCD factorization and renormalization scales, the choice of PDF set, as well as $\alpha_s$\@. For QCD scale uncertainties, the renormalization scale ($\mu_{R}$) and factorization scale ($\mu_{F}$) are varied independently up and down by a factor of two resulting in seven combinations. The envelope of the maximum deviations from the nominal yields is taken as the QCD scale uncertainty. For the $WZ$ samples, only the shape of the distributions is considered since the normalization is determined in the final fit.

For the \wh{} process, the nominal PDF set used is PDF4LHC, which provides 40 Hessian-reduced variations. An additional uncertainty associated with parton shower modeling is evaluated by comparing the nominal sample showered with Pythia8 to an alternative sample showered with Herwig7. This two-point comparison is symmetrized to define the showering uncertainty.

For both diboson and triboson processes, the theoretical uncertainties associated with the QCD scale and $\alpha_{s}$ are evaluated following the prescription used for the \wh{} process. The nominal PDF set used is NNPDF3.0 at NLO, with $\alpha_{s} (m_Z) = 0.118$. This set includes 100 variations, and the standard deviation is taken as the PDF uncertainty. The uncertainty related to parton showering is estimated through a two-point variation of the resummation scale (``MUQ''), where the scale is varied up and down by a factor of two relative to the nominal. Additionally, an uncertainty due to the choice of jet merging scale (``CKKW'') is derived by comparing the nominal sample which is generated with a 20~GeV merging scale, to samples produced with 15~GeV and 30~GeV merging scales. This uncertainty is symmetrized to produce the up and down variations. No parton showering uncertainty is assigned to the triboson samples due to the unavailability of alternative samples.

Only the normalizations of the minor contributions from ttV and ttH processes are constrained with relative cross section uncertainties $\frac{\Delta\sigma_{ttV}}{\sigma_{ttV}}$ = 13\% and $\frac{\Delta\sigma_{ttH}}{\sigma_{ttH}}$ = 10\%.
