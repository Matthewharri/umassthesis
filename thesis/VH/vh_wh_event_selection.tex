The \zdom{} channel is dominated by backgrounds that contain $Z$ bosons. To suppress such contributions, a $Z$-veto is implemented, requiring that the invariant mass of all SFOS lepton pairs, $\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}$, satisfy $|\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell} - \mathrm{m}_{Z}| > 25$ GeV. This selection targets backgrounds from $WZ^{*}/W\gamma^{*}$ and $ZZ^{*}$ processes by requiring that the di-lepton mass is sufficiently far from the $Z$ pole. This effectively removes on-shell $Z$ bosons leaving only off-shell $Z$-bosons. Events with neutrinos in their final state are selected by requiring that the \met{} significance $\metsig > 2.4$. Backgrounds with non-prompt leptons, such as those from \ttbar{} decays where two prompt leptons are accompanied by a third originating from a $b$-tagged jet, are suppressed by vetoing events containing at least one $b$-tagged jet. Additionally, leptons originating from $J/\psi$ and $\Upsilon$ decays are suppressed by requiring that the smallest invariant mass among the SFOS lepton pairs is greater than 12 GeV. Events containing four leptons, where the fourth lepton passes a looser selection requirement, are vetoed to ensure orthogonality between the \wh{} and \zh{} analyses, as well as to suppress background from $ZZ$ production. The \wh{} signal is further separated from the dominant background processes of $WZ^{*}/W\gamma^{*}$ and $ZZ^{*}$ through the use of an ANN\@. In this category, the ANN consists of three distinct output nodes, $o_{i}$, where each node describes the probability that an input event stems from process $i$, with $i$ corresponding to \wh{}, $WZ^{*}/W\gamma^{*}$, or $ZZ^{*}$.

The \zdep{} category is dominated by three main backgrounds consisting of $t\bar{t}$, $WWW$, and $WZ^{*}$. It may be surprising to see that the $WZ^{*}$ process is still a major background in this signal region even with the no SFOS lepton pair requirement, however, this happens due to $Z^{*} \to \tau^{+}\tau^{-}$ decays, where the $\tau$-leptons decay leptonically, producing different flavored leptons in the final state. In this channel, there is only one additional cut used after the pre-selection, which is the $b$-tagged jet veto to reduce the non-prompt background from $t\bar{t}$ decays. Similarly to the \zdom{} category, an ANN is used to separate the \wh{} signal from the dominant background processes of $t\bar{t}$, $WWW$, and $WZ^{*}$. In this channel, however, there are four output nodes, each one describing the probability that an event belongs to one of the previously mentioned processes.
