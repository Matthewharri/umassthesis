In the $WH$ channel, fake leptons --- leptons that are reconstructed by the detector, but do not necessarily originate from the hard-scatter interaction --- primarily arise from processes such as $V+$jets, $V\gamma$, and $t\bar{t}$. These backgrounds are estimated using data driven methods, in particular, a modified fake factor method~\cite{ATLAS:2019cbr} is applied for fake muon estimation, while a scale factor method is used for fake electron estimation. 

Requiring a minimum angular separation between leptons is found to be effective in suppressing fake lepton backgrounds. Leptons from $WH$ decays are rarely found to have $\Delta{R}_{\ell\ell} < 0.1$, whereas events with close-by leptons are more common in fake-lepton background events. Thus, as an optimization in reducing fake lepton contributions, a cut of $\Delta{R}_{\ell\ell} > 0.1$ is applied across the signal regions. Events containing two fake leptons are exceptionally rare, and are not included in this estimation.

A fake/scale factor is determined using pairs of CRs designed to enhance fake lepton contributions: the ID CR and the anti-ID CR\@. The ID CR selects events with three leptons, passing nominal lepton selection criteria for the $WH$ analysis, while the anti-ID CR selects two leptons passing nominal lepton selection criteria, and one that passes the anti-ID lepton selection, to enhance the fake contribution. The specific selection criteria for the ID and anti-ID leptons are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:wh_id_antiid_selection}.
