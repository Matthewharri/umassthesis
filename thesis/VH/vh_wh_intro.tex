This section details the categorization of signal events and background estimations for the \wh{} analysis.

The \wh{} analysis requires exactly three isolated leptons (muons or electrons, including those from leptonic $\tau$ decays) with $\pt{} > 15$ GeV and total charge $\pm{e}$, with at least one being matched to the triggering lepton. These requirements target backgrounds that could arise from multiple misidentified leptons, originating from $W+$jets production or $b\bar{b}$ production, and makes them negligible.

When the Higgs bosons decays into two $W$ bosons, the opening angle between the two resulting leptons is small compared to the angle between either of them and the lepton of the associated boson, due to helicity conservation and the V-A nature of the weak interaction. Due to this topology, a lepton labelling scheme is developed where the leptons are classified by identifying \lzero{} as the lepton with unique charge, \lone{} is the lepton closest in $\Delta{R}$ to \lzero{}, and \ltwo{} the remaining lepton. The \lzero{} lepton is guaranteed to be from the Higgs boson decay due to the charge requirement of $\pm{e}$, and \lone{} is also likely from the Higgs boson decay due to the spin correlations. Previously, it has been verified with MC simulation that \lzero{} is chosen correctly 99\% of the time, and \lone{} 85\% of the time.

In order to separate regions populated with backgrounds from $Z$ bosons, such as $WZ/W\gamma^{*}$, $ZZ^{*}$, and $Z\gamma{}$, the analysis separates events with at least one same-flavor opposite-sign (SFOS) lepton pairs from those with none. The region with these pairs contains about 3/4 of the signal, but suffers from this irreducible background. The pair without any SFOS pairs contains 1/4 of the signal, but needs stringent lepton identification to reduce backgrounds. Events containing at least one SFOS are classified as being in the \zdom{} category, while those without any in the \zdep{} category.

Backgrounds in this analysis are estimated using several different methods. The first category includes prompt processes such as $ZZ$, $VVV$, $ttV$, and $tZ$, where all selected leptons originate from electroweak boson decays, and are estimated directly from MC simulation.

The second category is a hybrid method used to estimate the main background process of the analysis, $WZ/W\gamma^{*}$. In this method, the $WZ/W\gamma^{*}$ background is estimated using MC simulation, but corrected with normalization factors (NFs), derived using a data-driven approach using a dedicated control region (CR) targeting this background. This will be presented in more detail in Section~\ref{sec:vh_wh_wz_control_regions}.

The final category includes backgrounds that contain non-prompt leptons originating from processes such as $Z+$jets, $Z+\gamma$, and $t\bar{t}$. These processes are not modelled well by MC simulation in rate or shape, and are therefore estimated using data-driven techniques detailed in Sections~\ref{sec:vh_wh_fake_lepton_estimation}. 

Dedicated artificial neural networks are used in this analysis to enhance sensitivity with respect to a purely cut-based analysis. The \zdom{} and \zdep{} channels have different neural networks and their trainings are explained in detail in Section~\ref{sec:vh_wh_nn_training}.
