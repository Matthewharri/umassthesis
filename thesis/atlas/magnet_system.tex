A crucial component of any particle physics detector is its magnet system, which bends charged particle trajectories to determine their momentum and charge. ATLAS uses two types of superconducting magnet systems, solenoidal and toroidal.

The central solenoid~\cite{atlas_central_solenoid} surrounds the ID and measures 5.8 m in length and 2.56~m in diameter, and generates a uniform 2 T magnetic field. The barrel and end-cap toroids~\cite{atlas_barrel_toroid, atlas_endcap_toroid} create a magnetic field for the MS that is highly inhomogeneous and can reach up to 3.5 T. The barrel toroids are the largest ones ever constructed and consist of 8 coils spanning 25.3 m, while each end-cap has a 10.7 m diameter.

All magnets operate at 4.5 K to provide the strong magnetic fields that are required.
