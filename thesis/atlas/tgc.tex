TGCs provide both a fast muon triggering ability and a measurement of the azimuthal coordinate in the region $1.05 < |\eta| < 2.4$. They are arranged in seven layers that sandwich the MDT chambers and are grouped into a triplet (three chambers) and two doublet layers (two chambers) forming a unit, as seen in Figure~\ref{fig:tgc_chamber}.

\begin{figure}[htp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{figures/atlas/atlas_tgc_chamber.png}
    \caption{Cross section of a TGC triplet and doublet module. The triplet module consists of three planes of anodes, while the doublet module consists of two planes of anodes. Taken from~\cite{atlas_collaboration_paper}.}\label{fig:tgc_chamber}
\end{figure}

Each chamber consists of gas volumes with a wire plane between two cathodes. TGCs are multi-wire proportional chambers with a 1.4 mm wire-to-cathode distance and 1.8 mm wire-to-wire spacing, allowing for 99\% of signals to arrive within 25 ns. They operate in a quasi-saturated mode using a gas mixture composed of 55\% $\mathrm{CO}_2$ and 45\% of $\mathrm{n-C_{5}H_{12}}$. In total the TGCs have 400,000 readout channels.
