This thesis presents a measurement of the Higgs boson production cross section via associated \wh{} and \zh{} production, using the $H \to WW^{*} \to \ell\nu\ell\nu$ decay channel. The analysis is based on $pp$ collision data collected by the ATLAS detector between 2022 and 2024, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 165.4~fb$^{-1}$ at a center-of-mass energy of $\sqrt{s} = 13.6$~TeV. At the time of writing, only the \wh{} channel is unblinded; therefore, the observed results presented here refer exclusively to \wh{} production. The product of the \hww{} branching fraction and the \wh{} production cross section is measured to be $0.26^{+0.13}_{-0.11}(\text{stat.})\pm{0.05}(\text{sys.})$~pb, in agreement with the Standard Model prediction of $0.311\pm0.006$ pb.

The Run~3 results are additionally interpreted within the STXS framework, where the measured signal strengths are found to be consistent with the Standard Model expectations. These results demonstrate a significant improvement in sensitivity in the lower \ptw{} bins compared to previous published ATLAS measurements in other channels, namely the $VH(b\bar{b})$ channel, highlighting the unique complementarity of the $WH \to WW^{*}$ final state.

Looking ahead, several avenues exist to further improve the analysis. As additional data are collected during Run~3, the statistical uncertainties will continue to decrease, leading to a more precise measurement. In parallel, ongoing developments in analysis techniques, such as improved non-prompt lepton rejection tools, have the potential to enhance the overall sensitivity. Furthermore, improvements in lepton identification could be achieved beyond the current labeling scheme. At present, the lepton originating from the associated $W$ boson is correctly identified approximately 85\% of the time, resulting in a 15\% contamination from other leptons. This misassignment directly impacts the modeling of the reconstructed \ptr{}, which is a key observable in the STXS interpretation. Finally, once the \zh{} channel is unblinded, a combined measurement of the \wh{} and \zh{} channels will enable a more comprehensive study of associated Higgs boson production and provide a more stringent test of the Standard Model.

In addition, a dedicated analysis to measure the $WWW$ production cross section is currently under development. Given the excess of events observed in regions sensitive to $WWW$ production in this analysis, such a measurement is expected to yield an interesting and complementary result.
