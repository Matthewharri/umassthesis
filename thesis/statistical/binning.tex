The choice of binning for discriminating variables plays a critical role in high-energy physics analyses, as it can significantly influence the overall sensitivity. While finer binning improves signal-to-background separation in the most relevant regions, an excessive number of bins may lead to statistical instabilities. Conversely, arbitrary or manually chosen binning schemes can introduce biases or yield suboptimal results. A more robust strategy is therefore to employ an algorithm that determines the binning automatically.

The general framework for remapping histograms prior to the final fit is defined in Equation~\ref{eq:bin_remap}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bin_remap}
  Z(I[k, l]) = Z(z_{s}, n_{s}(I[k, l]), N_{s}, z_{b}, n_{b}(I[k, l]), N_{b}),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{itemize}
\item $I[k, l]$ denotes an interval of the histogram spanning bins $k$ through $l$,
\item $N_{s}$ is the total number of $WH$ signal events in the histogram,
\item $N_{b}$ is the total number of background events in the histogram,
\item $n_{s}(I[k, l])$ is the number of $WH$ events contained in interval $I[k, l]$,
\item $n_{b}(I[k, l])$ is the number of background events in interval $I[k, l]$,
\item $z_{s}$ and $z_{b}$ are tunable parameters of the algorithm.
\end{itemize}

Among several possible transformation functions for the discriminant, this analysis adopts the form shown in Equation~\ref{eq:bin_transform}, which has been demonstrated in earlier ATLAS publications to provide a suitable balance between sensitivity and bin granularity:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bin_transform}
  Z = z_{s} \frac{n_{s}}{N_{s}} + z_{b} \frac{n_{b}}{N_{b}}.
\end{equation}

The algorithm proceeds as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Starting from the rightmost bin of the histogram, extend the interval $I[k, l]$ leftward one bin at a time, performing the re-binning from right to left,
\item At each step, compute the value of $Z$ for the current interval,
\item Once $Z([k_{0}, l]) > 1$, merge all bins in $I(k_{0}, l)$ into a single bin,
\item Repeat the procedure beginning with the bin immediately to the left of $k_{0}$, continuing until the first bin is included.
\end{enumerate}

This re-binning strategy is applied to both $WH$ channels in the inclusive measurement as well as the STXS analysis. The final choices of $z_{s}$ and $z_{b}$ for each channel are given in Table~\ref{tab:wh_zs_zb_parameters}.
